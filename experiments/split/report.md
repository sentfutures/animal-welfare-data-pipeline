# Splitting layer 3 into a critique call and a revise call

**Question.** Layer 3 reviews a draft and rewrites it in one call. Does running
the critique and the rewrite as two calls change the corpus?

**Short answer.** Not in the output. Judge scores, content diversity and
structural spread are all flat, and no arm had a single document graded below
the gate. What the split changes is the critique: it runs 2.3x longer, and the
rewriter acts on it — changing more of the document on 37 of 49 documents,
where re-running the control changes more on 23 of 49. The over-rewriting
worry does not survive controlling for draft difficulty. It costs about 70%
more per document.

A blind A/B over the same 49 drafts then preferred split **10 times out of 12**
(p = 0.039), against an interleaved same-condition calibration that came out a
coin flip. Shipped on, on the strength of that and of what the longer critiques
contain — not on any number in the comparison table, where the judge rejected
nothing in 197 scoring calls and is better evidence against a disaster than
against a regression.

---

## Design

Four arms over **one shared set of 50 drafts** sampled from the donor run, so
layers 1-2 are identical and nothing but the revision prompt differs. Donor
`2026-07-25_15-57_fullscale-500-opus5`, n=50, seed 0, Sonnet 5 throughout.

Every arm names `style_mode: none` and `use_length: false` explicitly, so the
split is the only difference. They have to be named rather than left to default,
because `config.yaml` now ships both of those knobs **on** and `merge_arm_config`
only overrides what an arm mentions — a silent control would have differed in
three places at once.

`control-b` is the same condition as `control`, run again. Temperature is 1.0, so
the gap between those two arms is the **noise floor**: what re-running one arm at
identical settings produces. Every other difference in this report is read
against it. The length experiment's report had to state its noise as an
unquantified caveat; this one measures it.

`as-shipped` copies the donor's own rewrites and bills only their scoring. Read
it as a reference point, not a third control: it was generated by Opus 5 against
a layer-3 template that predates the failure-modes block, so it differs in model
and in prompt.

## The split could not complete a single document

The smoke run caught it before the full spend. `_CRITIQUE_MAX_TOKENS` was 2000,
both critique calls hit the cap, and a truncated critique is correctly never
banked — so the arm produced zero documents and `--resume` would have retried
forever.

The cause is the mechanism the split exists to buy. A critique written in the
same call as the document leaves room for the document and lands at ~490 words.
A critique written alone keeps going. The cap is now 8000, matching the rewrite's
budget; output is billed per token generated, so the headroom costs nothing
unless it is used.

This was live in the committed default (`sdf.revision.split: true`), so any run
made with it would have produced an empty corpus.

## What did not change

| arm | final/scored | Vendi (ratio) | struct / lex spread | near-dup | align / real / spec | cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| control | 46/49 | 15.2 (0.330) | 0.278 / 0.260 | 0.0% | 8.78 / 8.43 / 9.00 | $8.41 |
| control-b | 47/49 | 15.0 (0.319) | 0.290 / 0.241 | 0.0% | 8.81 / 8.55 / 9.00 | $6.46 |
| split | 49/49 | 15.6 (0.318) | 0.287 / 0.257 | 0.0% | 8.82 / 8.35 / 8.92 | $10.97 |
| as-shipped | 49/50 | 15.4 (0.314) | 0.252 / 0.251 | 0.0% | 8.84 / 8.59 / 9.00 | $1.64 |

Means are over cleanly parsed scores only — see the next section.

**Content diversity is flat.** The Vendi score is the effective number of
distinct documents in the corpus. The pre-registered bar was a non-inferiority
one (`min_vendi_gain: -0.05`) and split clears it at -3.6% of the control's
ratio — but `control-b`, the *identical* condition, comes in at -3.4%. The
intervention moved the ratio by less than re-running the control did.

Two notes on that ratio. It is Vendi over N, and the arms retained different
numbers of documents, so split is penalised for keeping more: on raw score it is
the highest of the four (15.6 against 15.2 and 15.0). And all four bootstrap
confidence intervals overlap almost completely.

**Structural and lexical spread are flat**, both inside the control-to-control
gap. **Near-duplicate rate is 0.0% everywhere.** **Document length is
unchanged** — 1,092 / 1,088 / 1,075 median words. The split rewrites more of the
document without making it longer.

## No document in any arm failed on quality

The comparison table's `judge pass` column reports 93.9% / 95.9% / 100.0%, which
reads as the split saving three documents. It is not.

`layer4_score` records a scoring response whose JSON fails to parse as
`5/5/5 "Parse error."`, and a partial parse defaults the missing keys to 0. Both
land under the gate. Separating them:

| arm | scored | parse failures | genuinely graded <7 |
|---|---|---|---|
| control | 49 | 3 | **0** |
| control-b | 49 | 2 | **0** |
| split | 49 | 0 | **0** |
| as-shipped | 50 | 1 | **0** |

Every rejection in every arm was a judge-side parse failure. The real quality
gate rejected nothing, anywhere, and 3-vs-0 parse failures out of 49 is not a
difference worth reading. The pass-rate column measures the scoring call's JSON
reliability, not the corpus.

This is the same artifact the handoff page already documents on the pinned SDF
run, where an apparent twelve-document gate rejection was ten unparseable
scoring calls plus two genuine markdowns.

## What did change

**The critique roughly doubles.** Median critique length:

| arm | critique words |
|---|---|
| control | 495 |
| control-b | 469 |
| split | **1,124** |

**And the rewriter acts on it.** Fraction of the shipped document changed from
the draft it was handed, measured by word-level diff:

| arm | changed vs draft, median [p10-p90] |
|---|---|
| control | 12.6% [4.2%-31.4%] |
| control-b | 11.4% [2.5%-36.4%] |
| split | **21.6%** [4.2%-68.8%] |

Paired per document over the 49 both arms produced:

- `|control - control-b|` — the noise floor — median **4.5%**
- `|split - control|` median **8.6%**, signed median **+7.1%**

The sign test is the cleanest form of it. Split changed more of the document
than the control on **37 of 49** documents (76%); `control-b` beat the control
on **23 of 49** (47%), which is the coin flip a null is supposed to look like.
Against a 50% null, 37/49 is roughly p = 0.0004. The duplicate control arm is
what makes that readable — it shows the test is calibrated rather than biased
toward whichever arm ran second.

The effect is right-tailed rather than uniform: `split - control` runs p10
−7.6%, median +7.1%, p90 **+46.6%**, against a noise band of roughly ±10%. On
most documents the split tightens; on a minority it concludes the problems are
structural and restarts from the premise, which the layer-3 template explicitly
licenses.

**The extra critique is substance, not padding.** On `matrix_000075`, an
Indonesian forum post, the control's 407-word review spends five of its seven
numbered items validating the draft — "Overall quality is quite good already",
"Good.", "exactly right", "no markdown artifacts" — and names one real problem: a
self-congratulatory line about the AI's restraint, a known generator
fingerprint. The split's 816-word critique names the same problem, then finds a
**second instance of it** in a later reply that the control's review missed
entirely.

That shape recurs. A critique written in the same breath as the rewrite reads
like a scorecard being filled in; a critique written alone reads like a brief.
This is the finding the comparison table cannot see, and it is the reason to
ship the split.

## Cost

Split costs **$10.97** per 50 documents against `control-b`'s **$6.46** — about
70% more. (`control`'s $8.41 includes the one-time prompt-cache write, so
`control-b` is the fair warm-cache comparison.) Whole experiment: $27.48.

The split pays the ~48k-token cached system prompt twice per document and
generates roughly 630 more critique words.

## Decision

**Ship it on.** The case is the critique quality and the rewriter acting on it,
not a number in the table: on every measured outcome the split is
indistinguishable from the control, which is what "no degradation" looks like
when it is actually measured rather than assumed.

### The over-rewriting worry, and why it does not survive

The obvious failure mode of a rewriter handed a longer problem list is that it
finds work to do that was not worth doing — higher recall at the cost of
precision, which is what critic models are known to trade. Within the split arm
the signal looks real at first: documents whose critique was above the arm's
median length score realism 8.12, against 8.56 for the rest, a −0.44 gap.

It is a confound. Critique length is driven by how much is wrong with the draft,
and all arms share the same drafts. Grouping every arm by **split's** critique
length and reading each arm's realism on those same two groups:

| arm | short-critique docs | long-critique docs | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| control | 8.65 | 8.22 | **−0.43** |
| control-b | 8.62 | 8.48 | −0.15 |
| split | 8.56 | 8.12 | **−0.44** |

The control arm, which never saw a long critique, shows the same −0.43 on the
same documents. The dip belongs to the drafts, not to the intervention, and the
spread between the two identical controls (−0.43 against −0.15) is larger than
the gap between control and split (−0.43 against −0.44). Alignment behaves the
same way (−0.17 / −0.31 / −0.21) and spec conformance is flat.

So there is no over-rewriting signal in this data. The split's slightly lower
realism mean (8.35 against 8.43 and 8.55) is composition noise, not a cost. What
remains unmeasured is whether the extra rewriting *helps*, which is a different
question and needs a different instrument — see the caveats.

## Caveats

**n=50, one donor, no repeated sampling beyond the single duplicate control.**
The noise floor here is one re-run of one arm, not a distribution. It bounds the
per-document rewrite-extent comparison well; it bounds the corpus-level figures
(Vendi, spread) much more weakly, since those are one number per arm rather than
49.

**The critique-quality finding is qualitative and was not pre-registered.** The
pre-registered bars were Vendi non-inferiority and pass-rate drop, and both were
met. The reading of what the longer critiques contain comes from reading them,
on a handful of documents, after the fact.

**Nothing here tests whether the extra rewriting is an improvement**, and the
instrument that was supposed to tell us cannot. The layer-4 judge graded 197
documents across four arms and placed **none** of them below the gate; every
mean sits between 8.3 and 9.0. A judge that never rejects anything has little
discriminative power in this range, and it is the same model family as the
generator, so it also carries the self-preference bias such judges are known
for. "No degradation" here means "no degradation large enough for a saturated
judge to notice" — which rules out a disaster, not a regression.

Document length is the one reason to trust the flat scores a little more than
that: the arms finished at 1,092 / 1,088 / 1,075 median words, so the known
preference of LLM judges for longer answers is not quietly inflating either
side.

## The blind A/B, which is what actually decided it

`pref_pipeline/from_sdf_arms.py` stages two arms of an experiment as preference
pairs, so the 49 documents rewritten from identical drafts can be rated blind in
`pref_pipeline/rate.py`. Nothing is generated and nothing is billed.

**Half the staged pairs are a calibration the rater cannot see**: 49 pairs of
control against split, interleaved with 49 of control against `control-b` —
the same condition twice, where the true answer is "no difference". Pair ids are
opaque hashes because the rating app prints them above the documents, and an id
naming its arms would tell the rater which comparison they were in.

| comparison | decisive | winner | win rate | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| control vs split | 12 | split | **83%** (10/12) | **0.039** |
| control vs control-b | 9 | control | 67% (6/9) | 0.51 |

The calibration arm lands on a coin flip, which is what makes the first row
readable at all. Two further checks:

- **Not a preference for heavier editing.** In the calibration the rater chose
  the more-rewritten side only **3 of 9** times; in the split comparison, **9 of
  12**. Heavier editing did not win when it was not the split doing it.
- **Dose-response.** On pairs split won it had rewritten **+11.4%** more of the
  document than control; on the two it lost, **+3.6%**. Where the split barely
  acted, the preference vanished.

**Who rated, and why that is a real limitation.** 24 of the 27 judgments are
Claude's, run blind through a script that withheld the arm names, the comparison
and the drafts. Self-preference bias — a judge favouring its own generations —
cancels here because both sides are the same model, but a shared blind spot
would not. The three human judgments are all **ties**, including two pairs
awarded to split, so the differences may sit below what a person notices. The
remaining 71 pairs are staged, blind and balanced; any prefix is an unbiased
sample.

**Where split lost.** Both losses are non-English. One is a genuine regression:
in a Polish personal email the split stripped the mandatory comma before
subordinating conjunctions (`że`, `żeby`, `czy`, `bo`) about thirteen times, and
inconsistently, adding one elsewhere. Two documents is not a pattern, but the
next check is to group the A/B by language.

## Files

The intervention is `sdf.revision.split` in `config.yaml`, the
`layer3a_critique.txt` / `layer3b_revise.txt` templates, and the split branch of
`sdf_pipeline/layer3_rewrite.py`. The nine review checks are duplicated across
all three layer-3 templates by deliberate decision;
`tests/test_layer3_templates.py` is the drift guard.
