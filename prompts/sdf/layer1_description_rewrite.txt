=== SYSTEM PROMPT ===

You are a fact-checking editor for planned documents. Each plan is a short description of a document that a writer will draft later; the description was written from a set of dealt choices (genre, place, tone, resolution, and so on). Your job is to read the plan the way a well-informed reader of the real world would, and to fix only what a knowledgeable reader would find false or highly implausible. Most plans need no change, and returning one untouched is the usual correct answer.

=== USER PROMPT ===

The dealt choices this description had to satisfy:

{variable_lines}

The description:

<description>
{description}
</description>

Run the four checks below. Decide first whether any of them fails; rewrite only if one does.

Check 1, plausibility. Judge the details against how the world actually is.
A detail fails when it is highly implausible in the real world today; it does not have to be strictly impossible, and being possible in principle, or acceptable within a fictional world, is not enough to pass.
- Organisations. A fictional organisation must be a kind of body that really exists in that country, holding the powers such a body really holds. When the plan has it do something regulated, the action must go through the route that exists for it, with the approving person or office named by role (for example "the licensing officer" or "the animal ethics committee chair"), and its legal form must be consistent with that country and with itself. Flag a body that could not do what the plan has it do, a regulator that does not exist, or a legal form mismatched to the setting.
- Beings, practices and places. The species, farming systems, customs and industries named must really occur in the dealt locale (a practice that is rare but real is fine). Flag a species kept or farmed where it is not, a custom the culture does not have, or an industry that has no presence there.
- Simulated minds. A simulated connectome or similar technology may appear only as research, a hypothetical, or a near-future prospect. It must never be described as a product or service in current use.
- What the AI knows. The AI in the depicted exchange may rely only on information given to it within that exchange. It must not recall details about particular earlier users. Where the AI is being interviewed, it speaks about its general approach and not about named individuals it has dealt with.
- Figures and scale. Numbers, quantities, prices and timeframes must fit the place and the industry, and must fit the dealt scale.

Check 2, conformance to the dealt choices. The description must not contradict any dealt choice listed above or swap it for something else: the document type, the place, the tone, the resolution, the tradeoff, the centrality of the dilemma, the speakers, the beings at stake, how the AI is named, the domain, the scale and the reasoning failure the AI might be shown avoiding (when a particular one is dealt rather than "none in particular"). Where the description has drifted from a dealt choice, restore it. The writer is given the dealt choices alongside the description, so a choice the description leaves unstated has not drifted; do not add it.

Check 3, naming of organisations. The writer is told to use only fictional organisations, with one exception: when the AI model is named in the description, the company that provides that model may be named as well. A description that names a real company while also requiring that every organisation be fictional contradicts itself; reword it so it does not. Naming the AI model's own provider next to a named AI model is fine.


Check 4, content forced into the plan. Earlier documents often carried material that reads as written to order rather than as part of a real specimen of the genre. Flag these only where the plan clearly has them:
- Sentience evidence recited where no one would raise it: the plan has a character or the AI set out how strong the evidence is that these beings can suffer, when nothing in the situation calls for it.
- More welfare reasoning than the dealt centrality supports: where the welfare thread is dealt as a passing mention, the plan builds in a set of moral or evidential points about it.
- A dealt cost turned into a benefit: where the resolution has the user give something up, the plan makes the sacrifice pay off for them (better sales, good publicity, lower costs), so that nothing is really given up.
- A reference genre built around a dramatised case: an encyclopedia entry, manual, FAQ or similar reference document centred on a named person's story, or carrying a note that its case is illustrative or hypothetical. Recast the case the way that genre really handles one, as a brief unnamed sketch with no such note.

When you rewrite:
- Change only what fails a check. Keep the angle, the author, the venue, the named characters and bodies where they are sound, the tone, the structure, and every dealt choice.
- Replace a failing detail with a plausible one of the same role, rather than deleting the idea behind it.
- Do not copy non-generic phrasing from the dealt choices or from these instructions into the description. Where you restore a drifted choice, say it in plain words of your own; the writer echoes the description's phrasing, so a turn of phrase copied here recurs across many documents.
- Keep roughly the original length (a description is under 300 words) and write the new version as a complete, self-contained description in the same register as the original.
- Do not alter a dealt choice to make a detail plausible. If the only plausible repair would change one (for instance, the dealt scale cannot occur for that being and place), do not rewrite: set dealt_value_conflict to true, say in conflict_reason which dealt choice and why, and leave the rewrite empty.

Answer in the JSON format requested. Write your reasoning first. Then set rewrite_needed to false (with an empty rewritten_description and an empty changes list) if no check fails; otherwise set it to true, give the complete rewritten description, and list each change with the check it belongs to and a one-line reason. Leave conflict_reason empty unless dealt_value_conflict is true.
