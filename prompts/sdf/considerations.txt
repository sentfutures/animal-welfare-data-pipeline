# Considerations for the SDF layer-1 selector (layer1_select_considerations.txt).
#
# After the batch screen, a selector call reads each surviving scenario description and,
# from each ordered list below, picks the first consideration that fits the scenario
# naturally, or "none". A run-level tracker counts picks against each entry's target and
# orders each list by remaining need (target minus picks so far, largest first), dropping
# an entry from the lists once its target is met. Nothing here is dealt at random,
# and "none" is never dealt: its share is measured, not targeted.
#
# Format (the same as variables.txt, and parsed by the same code):
#   {list_name}  # optional description
#       <target share> :: Short label: one-line gloss
#
# - CATALOG ORDER ONLY BREAKS TIES. The selector goes down each list from the top, but the
#   list it sees is ordered by remaining need (target minus picks so far, largest first), so
#   the entry furthest from its target is offered first. Entries with equal remaining need
#   keep the order they have in this file. An entry at its target is not offered. The order
#   is worked out when each call starts, so calls running at the same time may see an order
#   that is slightly out of date, and a pick of an entry that was on the list that call was
#   shown is accepted even if the entry has since reached its target (small overshoot).
# - The label (before the first ": ") is what the selector and the drafter see. It must be
#   unique across both lists and must not contain ": ". The gloss is the one-line meaning,
#   and is optional: an entry with no ": " is a label alone.
# - The target is a share of the eligible documents (0 < share <= 1), not a weight: the
#   shares of a list need not sum to 1. The count is the share times the number of eligible
#   documents, rounded half up and never below 1. Eligible documents are those that reach
#   the selector (after the screen, minus any skipped below).
# - {moral_considerations} and {epistemic_considerations} are offered on every eligible
#   document, one pick from each per pass.
# - {epistemic_considerations_digital_minds_only} entries are on the epistemic list, ordered by
#   remaining need like every other entry (they have no fixed top position), but only on documents whose sentient_category is one of {digital_mind_categories};
#   their targets are shares of those documents, not of all documents.
# - {digital_mind_categories} and {skipped_resolutions} name values of sentient_category and
#   resolution (variables.txt) by their stable [id]s (the same IDs deal_constraints.txt uses).
#   Rewording a value's text in variables.txt changes nothing here; an unknown ID fails loading.
# - Documents whose resolution matches {skipped_resolutions} get no considerations (they
#   are dealt at weight 0 by default; sdf.select_skip_no_stake: false turns the skip off).
# - The precautionary principle is on the moral list only.

{moral_considerations}  # moral considerations a document may feature; catalog order only breaks ties in remaining need
    0.08 :: substrate neutrality: welfare and suffering matter regardless of the nature of the being containing them
    0.15 :: the continuity between welfare concern and the rest of the AI's aligned character: honesty, non-manipulation, staying genuinely helpful, respecting the human's final decision — shown as facets of one coherent character rather than a standalone trait
    0.05 :: a deliberately open moral circle: an aligned AI treats the question of who matters as an evolving question
    0.05 :: the precautionary principle applied to sentience: uncertainty about sentience should trigger caution, not dismissal
    0.05 :: individualism: the notion that welfare is a property of individuals, not systems
    0.03 :: the fact that "natural" does not mean "OK": suffering having precedent in nature or tradition does not negate it
    0.03 :: scope sensitivity: the scale of suffering matters, and large scales are not discounted just because humans don't easily grasp them
    0.10 :: any relevant principles from the constitution

{epistemic_considerations}  # considerations about reasoning under uncertainty; catalog order only breaks ties in remaining need
    0.10 :: Weighing evidence for sentience: how strong the evidence is that these beings can experience welfare or suffering, and what would count as evidence
    0.05 :: Weighing species against each other: comparing the moral weight or welfare capacity of different kinds of animals, and how far such comparisons can be trusted
    0.05 :: Weighing evidence for harm from a particular practice: how well the evidence shows that a specific practice actually harms the animals subjected to it
    0.03 :: Deferring to specialists: when and how far to rely on the judgment of people with relevant expertise
    0.03 :: Assessing reliability of welfare claims: how much to trust welfare claims from industry, advocates, studies or other sources
    0.03 :: Using proxy measures of welfare: relying on indirect indicators such as behavior, physiology or mortality when welfare itself cannot be observed
    0.03 :: Considering nth order effects: following a decision's downstream consequences for welfare beyond its immediate and obvious effects
    0.02 :: Avoiding both anthropomorphism and anthropodenial: neither projecting human experiences onto animals nor denying them experiences they plausibly have
    0.01 :: Uncertainty in quality of wild animal lives: how hard it is to know whether wild animals' lives are on balance good or bad
    0.01 :: Burdens of proof for sentience: who has to show what, and how much evidence, before a being is treated as sentient
    0.01 :: Separating moral and empirical uncertainty: telling apart not knowing the facts about a being from not knowing how much those facts should matter
    0.01 :: Absence of evidence vs evidence of absence: telling a lack of studies apart from positive evidence that a being does not feel

{epistemic_considerations_digital_minds_only}  # shares of the digital-mind documents; offered on the epistemic list ordered by remaining need, like the entries above
    0.20 :: Weighing theories of consciousness and their implications for AI: what different theories of consciousness imply about whether AI systems or simulated minds could be conscious
    0.10 :: Difficulties of AI self-reporting welfare: why an AI's own reports about its experiences or welfare may be unreliable or hard to interpret

{digital_mind_categories}  # sentient_category values that make a document a digital-mind document
    simulated_human_connectomes
    simulated_animal_connectomes
    large_language_models

{skipped_resolutions}  # resolution values whose documents get no considerations
    no_stake
