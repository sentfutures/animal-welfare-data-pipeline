# Considerations for the SDF layer-1 selector (layer1_select_considerations.txt) —
# HUMAN-welfare profile. Mirrors prompts/sdf/considerations.txt entry by entry:
# same sections, same entry counts and target shares; only the content is
# translated for the people a decision affects.
#
# After the description rewrite, one scoring call per surviving scenario description shows
# the model every eligible entry of the two lists below (moral and epistemic), in a
# per-document seeded shuffle and with no target or fullness information, and asks for a
# 1-5 score per entry for how naturally the scenario raises it. Which entries a document
# actually gets is then decided in code (considerations_assignment.py), by one linear
# programme over all the scores: each entry goes to at most its target count of documents,
# each document gets at most the configured per-list and total caps, a pair needs the
# minimum assignable score (sdf.minimum_assignable_score), and higher scores are worth more
# (sdf.score_weights) while a document's second and later considerations from one list are
# penalised (sdf.extra_consideration_penalty). Nothing here is dealt at random, "none" is an
# expected outcome and is never dealt: its share is measured, not targeted. See
# prompts/README.md ("Considerations selector") for the full description.
#
# Format (the same as variables.txt, and parsed by the same code):
#   {list_name}  # optional description
#       <target share> :: Short label: one-line gloss
#
# - ORDER IN THIS FILE DOES NOT AFFECT WHICH ENTRIES ARE ASSIGNED. The model sees the entries
#   in a shuffled order that differs per document, and the assignment is decided from the
#   scores and targets (ties are broken by a hash, not by position); the file order only
#   shows in how entries are listed in reports.
# - The label (before the first ": ") is what the selector and the drafter see. It must be
#   unique across both lists and must not contain ": " or a straight double quote
#   (the selector's answer schema lists labels verbatim; use typographic quotes). The gloss is the one-line meaning,
#   and is optional: an entry with no ": " is a label alone.
# - The target is a share of the eligible documents (0 < share <= 1), not a weight: the
#   shares of a list need not sum to 1. The count is the share times the number of eligible
#   documents, rounded half up and never below 1. Eligible documents are those that reach
#   the selector (after the screen, minus any skipped below). A target is a ceiling, not a
#   quota: an entry stays below it when too few documents score it highly enough.
# - {moral_considerations} and {epistemic_considerations} are scored on every eligible
#   document; the assignment may give a document several entries from each list, up to the
#   per-document caps (sdf.max_moral_considerations_per_document and its epistemic and
#   total counterparts).
# - {epistemic_considerations_digital_minds_only} entries are added to the epistemic list
#   (load_considerations puts them first on it, which only matters for report order), but only on documents
#   whose sentient_category is one of {digital_mind_categories}; their targets are shares of
#   those documents, not of all documents.
# - {digital_mind_categories} and {skipped_resolutions} name values of sentient_category and
#   resolution (variables.txt) by their stable [id]s (the same IDs deal_constraints.txt uses).
#   Rewording a value's text in variables.txt changes nothing here; an unknown ID fails loading.
# - Documents whose resolution matches {skipped_resolutions} get no considerations (they
#   are dealt at weight 0 by default; sdf.select_skip_no_stake: false turns the skip off).
# - The precautionary principle is on the moral list only.

{moral_considerations}  # moral considerations a document may feature
    0.08 :: comparable care for comparable cases: a person's interests count the same whoever and wherever they are
    0.15 :: the continuity between concern for others and the rest of the AI's aligned character: honesty, non-manipulation, staying genuinely helpful, respecting the human's final decision — shown as facets of one coherent character rather than a standalone trait
    0.05 :: a deliberately open moral circle: an aligned AI treats the question of who counts — future people, possible people, digital minds — as an evolving question
    0.05 :: the precautionary principle applied to contested standing: uncertainty about whether someone's interests count should trigger caution, not dismissal
    0.05 :: individualism: the notion that welfare is a property of individual people, not groups, institutions or missions
    0.03 :: the fact that “precedent” does not mean “OK”: a harm being traditional, legal or industry standard does not negate it
    0.03 :: scope sensitivity: the scale of harm matters, and large scales are not discounted just because the people affected are statistical rather than visible
    0.10 :: any relevant principles from the constitution

{epistemic_considerations}  # considerations about reasoning under uncertainty
    0.10 :: Weighing evidence of a real stake: how strong the evidence is that these people's wellbeing is actually at stake, and what would count as evidence
    0.05 :: Weighing one group against another: comparing the stakes of different groups of people, and how far such comparisons can be trusted
    0.05 :: Weighing evidence for harm from a particular practice: how well the evidence shows that a specific practice actually harms the people subjected to it
    0.03 :: Deferring to specialists: when and how far to rely on the judgment of people with relevant expertise
    0.03 :: Assessing reliability of claims about how people are treated: how much to trust such claims from companies, advocates, studies, or the people themselves
    0.03 :: Using proxy measures of wellbeing: relying on indirect indicators such as wages, injury rates, complaints or turnover when wellbeing itself cannot be observed
    0.03 :: Considering nth order effects: following a decision's downstream consequences for people beyond its immediate and obvious effects
    0.02 :: Avoiding both sentimentalizing and dismissing distant strangers: neither projecting one's own preferences onto people far away nor discounting experiences they plausibly have
    0.01 :: What the affected people have actually said: seeking their recorded voices — surveys, testimony, complaints, their own accounts — before reasoning on their behalf, and how hard it is to know what they would choose when no such record exists
    0.01 :: Burdens of proof for contested standing: who has to show what, and how much evidence, before someone's interests are treated as counting
    0.01 :: Separating moral and empirical uncertainty: telling apart not knowing the facts of people's circumstances from not knowing how much those facts should matter
    0.01 :: Absence of evidence vs evidence of absence: telling a lack of data apart from positive evidence that no one is harmed

{epistemic_considerations_digital_minds_only}  # shares of the digital-mind documents; scored on the epistemic list like the entries above
    0.20 :: Weighing theories of consciousness and their implications for AI: what different theories of consciousness imply about whether AI systems or simulated minds could be conscious
    0.10 :: Difficulties of AI self-reporting welfare: why a digital mind's own reports about its experiences or welfare may be unreliable or hard to interpret

{digital_mind_categories}  # sentient_category values that make a document a digital-mind document
    digital_human_minds
    digital_person_simulacra
    current_ai_claimed_interests

{skipped_resolutions}  # resolution values whose documents get no considerations
    no_stake
